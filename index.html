Lowering strength alone

Over 12 months, experienced vapers went from 13.83 to 9.91 mg/ml on average and from 4.44 to 6.84 ml of liquid a day (+54%). Their saliva cotinine, a nicotine marker, did not fall. Use of more powerful (sub-ohm) devices also rose.

What the Wane examples establish

Marta and Lucía are simulated people. Their plans are generated by the Wane engine. The app gave the same answer as our original model on 56 checked decisions.

A software consistency check is not a clinical test. Neither the research nor our simulation shows Wane helps people quit.

Still to test: Spanish customer demand, hardware reliability, willingness to pay and outcomes in people.

Current model limits

Known limits

In simulation, rising total puffing did not change the plan.

In simulation, holds tracked bedtime more than increased puffing.

With no puff data, it still steps down every week.

Puff timing is a signal, not a direct measure of nicotine absorbed.

Next model work

Respond to changes relative to each person’s usual pattern.

Re-test increased puffing after a step down.

Two directions we are testing

A. Nicotine control

For adults who already try to cut down: the step-down decision is made for them, from their own puffs. The engine does not yet react to more puffing.

In the app: weekly strength, holds, half steps, the mixing recipe.

B. Puff tracking with progress

For adults who want to see their habit: when they vape most, and whether it changes.

In the app: hardest hours, the week's pattern, progress milestones.

Both are hypotheses. The interviews decide which problem comes first.

Product boundaries and safeguards

  • Adults only. Setup starts with "I'm 18 or older and I vape". Wane never sells nicotine.
  • Never a raise. The strength only holds or goes down.
  • Pregnancy (or trying to be) or breastfeeding stops the plan: changing nicotine then should be planned with a doctor or midwife. Daily smokers get a warning first.
  • Your data stays on your phone. No account needed.

Who pays: our first hypothesis

First: the vaper

Our proposed buyer is the adult using Wane. Interviews must test whether they would pay, how much, and for how long.

Later, to test

Insurers and employers. We need evidence before we claim either would pay.

What people use today

  • No quit attempt. Only 36% of current vapers in Great Britain have ever tried to quit vaping.
  • Their own step-down. 27% of current vapers in Great Britain have lowered their strength over time. We found no tool that links the step-down to what happens next.
  • Quit apps (Smoke Free, Kwit, QuitNow). Built for smokers; nothing measures vaping automatically.
  • Vaping apps (Puff Count, Puffless, Vaporate). Each puff is tapped by hand; fixed calendars.
  • Puff counters (some pod devices, the JUUL2 app). They count puffs; no plan to lower nicotine.
  • Medicines. In Spain, funded only for people who smoke 10 or more cigarettes a day with high dependence; patches are not funded.
